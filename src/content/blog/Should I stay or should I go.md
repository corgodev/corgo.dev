# Should I stay or should I go

Before we start I have a confession to make. I rewrote this first blog post of mine so many times that it could probably fill out at least two or three articles.
Why? Because cocktail known as "corgodev" is made of a few ingredients: 
- Perfectionism
- Impostor syndrome
- Procrastination
- [Chemical X](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PlpUABjD_p0)

So after some time I decided to screw it and start out with a messy little piece that is a simple... drumroll... list o pros and cons!

And the thing I'm considering today is - should I stay or should I go (far far away from my current job and hopefully into someplace where I'm fulfilled and don't start hating myself).

## List of pros

- A lot of money (relative to other jobs in my country)
- Fully remote
- Manager whom I respect and overall a very talented engineering department
- Job market isn't so hot right now
- I've been here for a few years already, so I'm familiar with the tech stack and can do my job reasonably well.

## List of cons

- I make ~20% less than I did when I started out (thanks to the dollar plummeting since then), so it's become comparable to what I could get as a senior devops somewhere else. Raise-wise it's also looking bleak since I'm not working on any critical product (devops life I guess).
- Work life balance is not a priority here. Some examples:
	- Our "UnLiMiTeD PtO" is not really unlimited, which I obviously knew. 
	  What I didn't know is that it's heavily frowned upon that you go over 20 days of PTO. My european ass can't even.
	- Meetings at 7:00PM my timezone, so that our LA-based director can join once in a blue moon.
	- People pushing commits on Saturday or Sunday or at 2:00 AM and this being applauded. I'm all for recognizing and prizing hard work but we need to set and enforce boundaries as well. 
- I don't care about our product that much. It's better than it was for me at some of the previous companies but in the end if someone gave me a choice to work on a game or interesting startup or continue to work on my employee's platform - I would choose former 99,9% of the time.
- I genuinely can't remember when was the last time I woke up excited to work here.
- It's stressful and they are pretty liberal when it comes to layoffs so it doesn't play well with my impostor syndrome.

## Soooo...

It's kinda hard to lie to myself when I compare these two - I should go.
So, what's been holding me back? I think it's mostly the fact that - A - Job market isn't that hot right now and B - I'm not a risky person and I fear that I won't be able to find another job ever again,  so I'm trying to save as much money as possible to feel comfortable leaving.

Which I know is stupid, since it's not like I can save enough to retire or something right?
And I did try the other option which is staying and try to come up with more interesting work that would excite it. Didn't work and I don't think that trying it again will change anything to be honest.

So I guess I should go.
